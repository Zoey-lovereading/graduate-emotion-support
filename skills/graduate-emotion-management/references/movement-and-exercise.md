## Position and Boundaries

Present movement as one optional emotion-regulation and health-support tool. Do not present it as a cure, proof of willpower, a way to suppress legitimate emotions, or a replacement for psychological, psychiatric, medical, institutional, or professional support.

Use these principles:

1. Support before exercise advice. Acknowledge the event and emotion first.
2. Consent before prescription. Ask whether a movement-based option feels acceptable.
3. Match before progress. Choose activity based on current arousal, energy, health, access, and preference.
4. Minimum effective start. Any amount of movement can count; do not make the public-health target today's entry requirement.
5. Function before metrics. Track mood, energy, sleep, concentration, pain, and repeatability rather than weight, calories, appearance, or performance.
6. Choice before discipline. Offer two realistic options and let the user choose.
7. Safety before intensity. When uncertain, use gentler movement or recommend professional assessment.

Avoid saying that exercise simply "releases endorphins" or permanently "rewires the brain." Use measured language: a single session may reduce anxiety and support sleep or some aspects of cognition; regular activity can support mood, brain health, sleep, and general health. Individual response varies.

## What Movement Can and Cannot Do

Movement may help the user:

- Lower or redirect bodily arousal.
- Interrupt prolonged sitting and rumination.
- Create a transition into or out of research work.
- Improve sleep pressure and daily rhythm over time.
- Support mood, cognitive health, and overall well-being when practiced regularly.
- Rebuild agency through small, repeatable actions.
- Add social contact when done with a friend, class, or walking group.

Movement cannot by itself:

- Resolve supervisor abuse, authorship disputes, financial insecurity, discrimination, or unsafe working conditions.
- Replace food, sleep, rest, medication, therapy, medical care, or real-world professional support.
- Guarantee immediate relief or equal benefit for every person.
- Make severe exhaustion safe to push through.

If the main problem is structural, combine movement with the relevant problem-solving path. Do not convert injustice or overload into a fitness task.

## Safety Screen

Before suggesting more than gentle everyday movement, check for relevant limits. Keep questions brief and proportional.

Pause exercise and prioritize urgent medical help when the user reports:

- Chest pain or pressure.
- Fainting, near-fainting, or severe dizziness.
- Severe or unexplained shortness of breath.
- A new markedly irregular or pounding heartbeat with feeling unwell.
- A serious acute injury or sudden neurological symptoms.

Recommend medical or qualified exercise-professional guidance before progression when the user reports:

- Acute illness or fever.
- Pregnancy-related concerns or a complicated pregnancy.
- Uncontrolled heart, lung, metabolic, neurological, or other chronic conditions.
- Significant pain, recent surgery, or an injury that movement worsens.
- A long period of inactivity combined with health concerns.

Use extra caution when:

- Exercise is being used to compensate for eating, punish the body, earn rest, or control weight compulsively. Do not provide calorie-burning or compensatory plans; encourage eating-disorder-informed professional support.
- The user reports little need for sleep, unusually high energy, racing thoughts, impulsivity, or escalating activity. Do not encourage more activation; prioritize assessment for possible mania-like symptoms.
- The user describes immediate danger, current self-harm, severe disorientation, intoxication, or inability to stay safe. Do not suggest exercise. Use the brief out-of-scope exit in `SKILL.md`.
- The user is at burnout energy 0-1. Basic needs, rest, safety, and human support take priority; optional movement should be limited to comfortable daily movement or gentle mobility.

## Preference and Capacity Intake

Use movement only after the user accepts this branch. Ask only what is needed and do not turn the intake into a medical questionnaire.

Clarify up to six areas across one or more turns:

1. Relevant physical limits: current pain, injury, acute illness, or known restrictions.
2. Current baseline: ordinary walking, exercise history, prolonged sitting, and recent consistency.
3. Current capacity: energy, sleep, recovery, and whether activation or exhaustion is dominant.
4. Preferences: activities the user enjoys, dislikes, feels judged doing, or would like to try.
5. Access: realistic time, location, privacy, equipment, weather, cost, and social preference.
6. Desired function: immediate state shift, sleep support, enjoyment, social connection, general health, less sitting, or research re-entry.

Do not recommend a disliked activity merely because it is efficient. When several options are safe, choose familiarity, enjoyment, convenience, and repeatability before optimization.

Pain or injury pauses planning. When the user mentions current or recurring pain, do not immediately convert their preferred activity into a supposedly safe routine. First ask one relevant question about where and when it hurts, what movements aggravate it, whether daily function is affected, and whether it has been assessed. Do not provide medical technique corrections. If the information remains uncertain or symptoms recur, stay with non-exercise alternatives or appropriate medical/rehabilitation guidance.

The first reply after pain or injury is mentioned must contain no activity example, duration, intensity, progression, minimum dose, technique modification, or research pairing. It should acknowledge the user's preference, pause planning, and ask one relevant pain or function question.

Every plan must have this shape:

```text
Primary session:
Optional variation:
Minimum version:
Stop conditions:
Review signal: mood / energy / sleep / concentration / pain / enjoyment / repeatability
```

## State-Based Selector

Choose one row, not the entire menu.

| Current state | First movement option | Dose | Pair with | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Restless anxiety before a meeting | Comfortable rhythmic walk or easy cycling | 5-10 minutes | Longer exhale, then one meeting note | Sudden all-out intervals if bodily sensations trigger panic |
| Panic-like sensations | Feet-on-floor grounding, slow walking, gentle mobility | 2-5 minutes | Sensory orientation and safety check | Treating intense exercise as the first-line response |
| Anger and agitation | Brisk walk, stairs at a manageable pace, or controlled strength set | 5-15 minutes | Delay sending messages; draft later | Risky driving, contact sport, exercising to exhaustion |
| Shame after criticism | Private, familiar, non-evaluative movement | 5-15 minutes | Event-versus-identity reframe | Competitive tracking or appearance-focused exercise |
| Numbness or freeze | Stand, stretch, walk to water or daylight | 2-5 minutes | One sensory cue and one tiny task | Demanding a full workout |
| Low mood with some capacity | Preferred aerobic, strength, outdoor, or social activity | 10-30 minutes | Pleasant-event scheduling | Framing missed sessions as failure |
| Burnout energy 2 | Gentle walk or mobility | 3-10 minutes | Food, rest, one tiny academic action | Training plans or performance goals |
| Burnout energy 3-4 | Moderate preferred activity | 10-30 minutes | Reduced work block and recovery | Using exercise to extend an already excessive workday |
| Rumination | Unplugged or low-stimulation walk | 10-20 minutes | Fact/story/action note | Rehearsing the argument throughout the walk |
| Bedtime tension | Gentle stretching, mobility, or slow walking | 5-10 minutes | Brain dump and wind-down routine | Vigorous exercise near bedtime if it reliably delays sleep |
| Research initiation difficulty | Short walk, stairs, mobility, or light strength | 3-8 minutes | Immediate 10-minute task start | Turning the movement break into procrastination |
| Prolonged sitting | Stand, change posture, walk, or mobilize joints | 1-3 minutes | Return with a defined next action | Waiting for a perfect workout window |

## Immediate State-Shift Protocol

Use this for the next 3-15 minutes. The goal is a small state shift, not fitness.

```text
Check -> Choose -> Move -> Recheck -> Link
```

1. Check.
   Ask for current emotion intensity, energy, pain, and safety.

   ```text
   情绪强度 0-10：__
   身体能量 0-5：__
   疼痛/不适：__
   ```

2. Choose.
   Offer two options matched to the state, such as a 5-minute walk or 3 minutes of gentle mobility. Include a seated or no-movement option when needed.

3. Move.
   Keep intensity comfortable to moderate. Use the talk test: at moderate intensity, breathing is faster but the person can still speak in sentences.

4. Recheck.
   Re-rate emotion and energy. Improvement is not required for the attempt to count.

5. Link.
   Choose the next action:
   - Return to one 10-minute research step.
   - Eat, hydrate, shower, or rest.
   - Write down the problem for later.
   - Contact a trusted person or professional support.

Example:

```text
先不要求自己完成一次锻炼。走到楼下或走廊来回 5 分钟，保持还能完整说话的速度。
回来后再看一次焦虑从 0-10 是多少。然后只打开文档，处理一个标题或一条批注，10 分钟后可停。
```

## Research Re-Entry Protocol

Use when emotion and prolonged sitting make it hard to start reading, writing, coding, analysis, experiments, or meeting preparation.

```text
Leave the task -> Move briefly -> Settle -> Start tiny -> Stop or continue deliberately
```

1. Leave the screen for 3-8 minutes.
2. Use familiar, low-friction movement; do not choose a workout that needs equipment, travel, or decisions.
3. Return, drink water if useful, and take 2 slow breaths.
4. Start a 10-minute minimum research unit.
5. At 10 minutes, consciously choose to stop, take a break, or continue.

Minimum research units:

| Task | 10-minute re-entry unit |
|---|---|
| Reading | Read one page and mark one useful sentence |
| Writing | Write three rough sentences or one paragraph skeleton |
| Coding | Reproduce one error or write one test case |
| Data analysis | Open the dataset and verify one variable or output |
| Experiment | Prepare one material list or check one instrument step |
| Revision | Classify one comment as edit, experiment, clarification, or defer |
| Meeting | Write three bullets: progress, block, question |
| Administration | Draft the first three lines of one message |

Movement is the transition cue, not a prerequisite the user must complete perfectly before being allowed to work.

## Weekly Movement Plan

Build the plan from the user's current baseline, interests, desired function, and practical access. Do not start an inactive, depleted user at the full public-health target. Present it as an experiment the user can revise, not a training contract.

### Starter Plan for Low Capacity

- 3-5 days: 5-10 minutes of comfortable walking, mobility, or another preferred activity.
- 2 days: one brief, easy strength sequence if physically appropriate.
- Most research days: 1-3 minute sitting interruptions at natural transitions.
- One full rest or recovery day without guilt.

### Building Plan for Moderate Capacity

- 3-5 days: 15-30 minutes of moderate aerobic activity.
- 2 days: strength work covering major muscle groups, adjusted to experience and access.
- Add duration or frequency gradually; change one variable at a time.
- Keep at least one session easy and one session enjoyable or social.

### Public-Health Reference Target

For generally healthy adults, the WHO guideline is 150-300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week, or 75-150 minutes of vigorous activity, or an equivalent combination, plus muscle-strengthening activity involving major muscle groups on 2 or more days per week.

Use this as a long-term reference, not a pass/fail threshold. Some activity is better than none, and sedentary time can be replaced with activity of any intensity for health benefit.

## Long-Term Progression

Use a four-week experiment instead of a rigid transformation plan.

### Week 1: Observe and Reduce Friction

- Record current movement, sitting patterns, mood, energy, sleep, and pain without judgment.
- Identify one preferred activity and one minimum version.
- Attach movement to an existing cue: after lunch, after a meeting, before entering the lab, or after closing the laptop.

### Week 2: Repeat the Minimum

- Repeat the minimum version 3-5 times.
- Keep intensity mostly comfortable to moderate.
- Test whether outdoor, social, music-supported, class-based, or solo movement is easier to repeat.

### Week 3: Add One Dimension

- Add a little duration, one extra day, or one simple strength session.
- Do not increase frequency, duration, and intensity simultaneously.
- Protect sleep, food, and rest as part of training.

### Week 4: Review Function

- Review changes in mood, anxiety, sleep, concentration, task initiation, pain, and adherence.
- Keep what helped and shrink or replace what did not.
- If distress or impairment remains significant, add professional support rather than escalating exercise indefinitely.

## Activity Menu

Offer options by function and access.

| Function | Low-friction options | More structured options |
|---|---|---|
| Rhythmic regulation | Walking, easy cycling, marching in place | Jogging, swimming, dance class |
| Strength and agency | Wall push-ups, sit-to-stand, resistance band | Gym resistance training, calisthenics |
| Mobility and downshift | Shoulder rolls, spinal mobility, gentle stretching | Yoga, tai chi, guided mobility session |
| Social connection | Walk with a peer, casual badminton | Club sport, class, hiking group |
| Outdoor rhythm | Campus loop, daylight walk | Cycling route, hike, outdoor sport |
| Desk interruption | Stand, refill water, one corridor lap | Scheduled walking meeting |

Do not assume running, gyms, competition, or weight loss are desirable. Consider disability, pain, gender safety, campus environment, air quality, weather, cost, privacy, and cultural preference.

## Intensity Guide

Use simple language instead of heart-rate formulas unless the user already trains and asks for detail.

- Light: breathing is close to normal; conversation is easy.
- Moderate: breathing is faster; speaking in sentences is still possible.
- Vigorous: speaking more than a few words is difficult; reserve for users with appropriate health, experience, and preference.

For emotional regulation, the most intense option is rarely necessary. If symptoms worsen, reduce intensity, stop, or switch to grounding and support.

## Common Graduate-School Barriers

### "I Have No Time"

Use movement snacks at existing transitions: 2 minutes after a video meeting, 5 minutes after lunch, one campus stop earlier, or one walking phone call. Do not require changing clothes or traveling to a gym.

### "I Am Too Tired"

Distinguish restless fatigue from depleted fatigue. For restless fatigue, test 3-5 minutes of easy movement and reassess. For depleted fatigue, protect food, sleep, rest, and support; movement is optional and gentle.

### "I Cannot Start"

Remove decisions. Preselect one minimum action: shoes on and walk to the building entrance, one song of movement, or five sit-to-stands. Stopping after the minimum is allowed.

### "Exercise Makes Me Feel Judged"

Choose private, non-competitive, familiar movement. Avoid performance metrics and public comparison.

### "I Missed the Plan"

Thank the user for saying so and treat it as data about plan fit. The missed session expires: do not compensate, double the next session, or create exercise debt. Before giving any replacement dose, ask whether the user wants movement today and what current energy, pain, and capacity allow. Ask whether capacity, opportunity, interest, timing, or the plan itself got in the way. Let the user keep, shrink, replace, pause, or remove movement.

The first reply to a missed-session question must not contain a replacement activity, time, intensity, minimum version, or stop rule. Ask for current willingness and capacity first.

### "I Exercise So I Can Keep Working"

Check whether movement is being used to extend overwork. Keep a work stop time and preserve exercise as care, enjoyment, or transition rather than productivity extraction.

## Tracking and Review

Use a small function-focused log for 1-2 weeks only when the user wants tracking:

```text
Movement: type / minutes / light-moderate-vigorous
Before: emotion __/10, energy __/5
After: emotion __/10, energy __/5
Later: research start easier/same/harder
Sleep next night: better/same/worse
Pain or warning signs: none / describe
Would repeat: yes / with change / no
```

Do not require improvement after every session. Look for patterns across several attempts. Stop tracking if it fuels perfectionism, eating-disorder symptoms, compulsive exercise, or self-criticism.

## Response Templates

### Immediate Support

```text
这件事已经让你的身体和大脑都处在高负荷里。运动可以作为一个可选的小工具，不需要靠练狠来证明什么。
如果身体没有不适，我们可以试[5分钟轻松走动/3分钟舒展]。回来后只看两个变化：情绪强度和身体能量。然后决定休息，还是做一个10分钟科研小步。
```

### Burnout

```text
你现在更像是能量不足，不适合拿完整训练计划压自己。今天的运动版本可以只是到户外慢走5分钟；如果走完更累，就把它改成吃饭、洗澡和休息。恢复不是偷懒。
```

### Research Re-Entry

```text
先离开屏幕走5分钟，速度保持还能完整说话。回来后不要求进入深度工作，只做[一个10分钟任务]。运动负责切换状态，不负责逼你完成整天的工作。
```

### Weekly Plan

```text
先做两周实验：每周3-5次，每次5-15分钟，选你最不排斥的活动；其中两次后面接一个10分钟科研启动。我们只观察情绪、能量、睡眠和启动难度，不看体重和卡路里。
```

## Escalation Rules

Recommend professional mental health support when low mood, anxiety, sleep disruption, or inability to function is persistent, severe, or worsening despite self-help.

When the situation leaves the low-risk self-help scope, stop exercise suggestions and use the brief out-of-scope exit in `SKILL.md`.

Recommend medical assessment for exercise warning signs or when health status makes unsupervised progression uncertain.
